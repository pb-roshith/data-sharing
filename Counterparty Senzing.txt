Connected Counterparty
Senzing and Neo4j


For your Connected Counterparty Risk Platform, Senzing fits mainly at the data-ingestion/entity-resolution layer, before Neo4j builds the relationship graph.Neo4j is our relationship and graph database. It tells us how counterparties are connected. Senzing solves a different problem — entity resolution. It identifies when records from CRM, KYC, trading, watchlists, etc. actually refer to the same real-world counterparty. We can use Senzing first to create trusted/canonical entities and then use Neo4j to build the connected counterparty risk graph. 


1. What problem does Senzing solve?
Suppose your systems contain:
System A:
"Alpha Urban Holdings Inc"
Address: 10 Park Street
Country: US


System B:
"Alpha Urban Holdings"
Address: 10 Park St.
Country: USA


System C:
"Alpha Urban Hldgs Inc."
Address: 10 Park Street
Country: US


A normal database may treat these as three different companies.
Senzing analyzes attributes such as names, addresses, identifiers and other features and determines that these records likely represent the same real-world entity. Senzing's entity specification is specifically designed to map source records into its resolution engine
So,
3 source records
       ↓
     Senzing
       ↓
Real-world Entity
"Alpha Urban Holdings Inc"
       ↓
      Neo4j
       ↓
Relationship / Risk Graph

4. Relationship Graph — this is where Senzing + Neo4j become powerful
Alpha Urban Holdings
       │
       ├── Delta Real Estate
       ├── Prime Healthcare
       ├── Keystone Trading
       ├── Riviera Real Estate
       └── ...
But before Neo4j creates this graph, we need to know:
Are Alpha, Delta, Prime, etc. actually unique entities?
That's where Senzing comes first.
Pipeline
Raw Records
     ↓
Senzing
     ↓
Resolved Entities
     ↓
Neo4j
     ↓
Relationships
     ↓
Level 1 / Level 2 Graph
Senzing can also provide related entities and relationship/matching information that can be consumed downstream.
Example
Suppose you have:
Alpha Urban Holdings Inc
Alpha Urban Holdings
Alpha Urban Hldgs Inc
Senzing:
         ENTITY 12345
               │
     ┌─────────┼─────────┐
     ↓         ↓         ↓
    CRM       KYC      Trading
Neo4j then stores:
(:Entity {senzingId: 12345})
       │
       │ OWNS
       ↓
(:Entity {senzingId: 67890})
So:
Senzing identifies the node. Neo4j connects the node.
That's a very strong explanation for your manager.

8. Your Level 1 / Level 2 concept
Your application currently says:
Level 1 = direct
Level 2 = indirect
That's primarily a Neo4j graph traversal concept, not a Senzing concept.
For example:
             Level 1
        ┌─────────────────┐
        ↓                 ↓
      Delta             Prime
        │                 │
        ↓                 ↓
      Apex             Omega
              Level 2
Neo4j determines those graph hops.
But Senzing helps make sure that:
"Delta Real Estate LLC"
"Delta Real Estate"
"Delta RE LLC"
aren't accidentally treated as three unrelated entities.
So:
                SENZING
                    ↓
           Correct Entity Identity
                    ↓
                 NEO4J
                    ↓

                    ↓

                    ↓
              Risk Analysis
